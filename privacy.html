Information we collect

Most of our apps are designed to work without accounts and without collecting personal information. We never sell your data, and we don't track you across other apps or websites. Where an app needs a specific permission — such as photos, location, or calendar — to do its job, that's explained in the app's own section below.

  • No accounts: we don't ask you to sign up, and we don't collect your name, email, or contacts.
  • On-device data: things like your scores, progress, settings, and preferences are stored locally on your device. Where you have enabled iCloud or a platform backup, some of this may sync through your own Apple or Google account — but it is never transmitted to us.
  • No advertising: our apps do not contain third-party ads and do not track you for advertising.
  • App-specific permissions: some apps need access to features like photos or your calendar to work. These are used only for that app's stated purpose — see below.

PDF Offline Studio (nothing leaves the device)

PDF Offline Studio collects nothing, transmits nothing, and has no server. That is not a policy decision that could quietly change — the app is built without permission to use the internet at all, so it is not able to send a document anywhere. You can check this yourself: on iOS and macOS it declares no network entitlement, and on Android it declares no INTERNET permission.

Your documents. When you open a PDF, the app copies it into a temporary working area on your own device so it can edit it without touching your original. That working area is wiped every time the app starts. Files leave the app only when you send them — to the Files app, to another app through the share sheet, or to a printer — and once a document has been handed to another app, that app's own policy applies to it.

Passwords. If you open a password-protected PDF and enter the password, the app makes a temporary unprotected copy so it can work on the pages. The password itself is held in memory only and is forgotten as soon as that copy exists. It is never written to disk, and there is nowhere for it to be sent.

What stays between sessions. Five things, all of them on your device, none of them describing a document:

  • Tools you have starred, so your favourites stay at the top of the home screen — a short list of tool names.
  • A signature, if you choose to save one. Held in the device keychain on iOS and macOS, or the equivalent encrypted store on Android, and removed when you delete it or uninstall the app.
  • One custom stamp, if you make one. Kept as a description rather than a picture — the words on it, an outline shape, and a few colours — so it can be redrawn at the right size for whatever page it lands on.
  • Your chosen language, if you pick one instead of following the device.
  • Two small numbers, used to decide when to ask you — once — for an App Store rating: how many times the app has been opened, and whether the question has already been asked.

Photos. Turning images into a PDF uses the system photo picker, which runs outside the app. The app receives only the images you chose and never gets access to your library.

No analytics, no crash reporting, no advertising or device identifiers, no location, and nothing at all about the contents, filenames, or metadata of your documents.

Call My Meeting (calendar access)

Call My Meeting asks for permission to read your calendar so it can set a local alarm before each meeting. Your calendar stays private:

  • Read on-device only: calendar events are read on your device to work out when to ring. They are never uploaded, sent to us, or shared with anyone.
  • No copies kept: the app does not store your events on a server. It only keeps what it needs on your device to schedule upcoming alarms.
  • Alarms are local: alarms and reminders are scheduled and fire entirely on your device.
  • Optional notifications: if you allow notifications, they are used only as an occasional on-device nudge to reopen the app. There is no push server.

You can turn Calendar, Alarms, or Notifications access on or off at any time in your device Settings.

Route Reel (photos & maps)

Route Reel turns your trip photos into a map-flyover video. To do that it needs your photos and the location saved inside them, and it uses a mapping service to draw the map and routes. Here's exactly how that works:

  • Photos & their location: with your permission, Route Reel reads photos from your device, including the GPS coordinates stored in each photo's metadata. These coordinates are what place your trip on the map. Photos are read on your device to build your reel and are never uploaded to us.
  • Why broad photo access: Route Reel reads photos directly (rather than through the system photo picker) because the picker removes the location metadata the app relies on. Access is used only to build your reel.
  • Maps & routes (Mapbox): to show the map and calculate routes between your stops, the coordinates of your stops are sent to Mapbox, our maps provider, over a secure connection. This is necessary for the core map feature. Mapbox's handling of that data is governed by its own privacy policy. Route Reel disables Mapbox's optional usage telemetry where the SDK allows.
  • Screen recording: to create the final video, Route Reel records the on-screen map animation it plays back. The resulting video is saved to your device's gallery and is not transmitted anywhere by the app.
  • Notifications: shown only to display the in-progress recording status, as required by Android for screen capture.

You can turn Photos, Location, or Notifications access on or off at any time in your device Settings.

Missed Flight (saves, tips & sharing)

Missed Flight is a single-player game with no accounts, and it works without collecting any personal information. A few features are worth explaining:

  • Game saves & iCloud: your coins, daily streak, settings, and unlocked content are saved on your device. If you have iCloud enabled, a small amount of this game data (such as your coin total, streak, and unlocked airports) is synced through Apple's iCloud Key-Value Storage so your progress carries across your own devices. That data goes to your own iCloud account under Apple's privacy policy — never to us.
  • In-app tips (purchases): the optional "Tip Jar" lets you support development. These purchases are processed entirely by Apple (App Store) or Google (Google Play). The app never sees or stores your payment details, and tips unlock no special content or gameplay advantage.
  • Optional notifications: if you allow them, the game may send an occasional on-device reminder to help keep your daily streak alive. These are scheduled and fire locally on your device — there is no push server. You can turn them off any time in Settings.
  • Sharing: when you tap Share, the game creates an image and a short caption on your device and hands them to the system share sheet for you to share if you choose. Nothing is uploaded to us.

Missed Flight does not access your photos, location, calendar, or contacts.

MealMetrics (photos, a food diary, and the parts that leave the device)

MealMetrics is the only app here that sends anything of yours to a service in order to work, so this section is longer than the others and says plainly what goes where.

Your food diary is yours. Everything you log — what you ate, when, the weights, the calculated nutrients, your height, weight, date of birth and activity level — is stored on your device. If you are signed in to iCloud, it also syncs through your own iCloud account, in its private database, so the same diary appears on your other devices and survives a new phone. That copy lives in Apple's care under Apple's privacy policy; we cannot see it, and we keep no copy of your diary on any server of ours. There is no account and no sign-up. Meal photographs are deliberately left out of that sync: they stay on the iPhone that took them.

What leaves the device, and when.

  • A meal photo, only when you ask for it to be identified. When you photograph a meal, the app sends one downsized copy (about 768 pixels on the long side) through our server to a large language model so it can name the food. It is sent to name the food and for nothing else. Our server passes it straight through and keeps no copy, no log of it, and nothing derived from it. The provider processes it under its own policy, which for API traffic is typically a short abuse-monitoring retention and no training on your content.
  • What you type, when you describe a meal. The sentence you write is sent the same way, to the same model, and treated the same way. So are short follow-ups about that meal — splitting a dish into ingredients, or filling in vitamins a food database does not list.
  • What you say, if you speak instead of typing. Your words are turned into text by Apple's speech recognition, which depending on your device and language happens on the iPhone or on Apple's servers under Apple's policy. The text is then treated exactly like a typed sentence. Nothing is recorded or kept.
  • A barcode number, or a product name you search for. Only the number or the words are sent, to Open Food Facts, to look up what the product is. The photograph of the barcode never leaves the device.

What never leaves the device: your food log itself, your body details, your history, and the photographs once a meal has been saved. Photos are kept in the app's own storage, not your photo library, and are deleted when you delete the meal.

The identifier. Because there are no accounts, the free trial is counted against a random identifier generated on first launch and kept in the device Keychain. It is a random number. It is not your Apple ID, your device ID, or anything that identifies you, and it is not shared with anyone. Our server stores only that identifier, a date, a count of how many meals and requests it has made, and how many words the model read and wrote for them — enough to know that a device has used some of today's fair-use allowance and what the service costs to run. No message, no photo, and no food name is ever written to our server's storage.

Proving the app is the app. Each request carries a short cryptographic token issued by Apple's App Attest service, which tells our server the request came from a genuine, unmodified copy of MealMetrics rather than from someone who found the address. The token says nothing about you and cannot be traced back to your Apple ID.

Apple Health, if you allow it. With your permission the app reads your weight and the energy you burn, so the day's reference figures follow how much you moved, and writes the meals and water you log so Health and your other apps can see them. Health data is read and written on the device, is never sent to our server or to the model, and is never used for advertising. You can turn either direction off in Health at any time.

Subscriptions. Handled entirely by Apple. To confirm you are subscribed, the app sends the signed receipt Apple issues for your purchase to our server, which checks Apple's signature on it. We never see or store your payment details, your name, or your email address.

Where it runs. Our server is in Frankfurt, Germany. Requests to the model provider may be processed outside the EU under that provider's own terms.

Starting over. "Start over" in Settings deletes your meals, drinks, recipes, saved foods and photos on the device and in your iCloud copy, and, if you choose, your height, weight and date of birth too. Anything already written to Apple Health stays there, and is yours to delete in Health.

MealMetrics is not a medical device and the figures it shows are reference values for a population, not advice about you. It does not score your food, set goals, or tell you what to eat.

Purchases & support

Some apps offer optional in-app purchases (for example, tips to support development). These are processed entirely by Apple (App Store) or Google (Google Play) under their own privacy policies — our apps never see or store your payment information. If you instead choose to support us via “Buy me a coffee,” that transaction is handled entirely by Buy Me a Coffee on their own website and under their own privacy policy.

Children's privacy

Our apps do not knowingly collect personal information from anyone, including children. If you believe a child has provided us personal information, contact us and we'll address it.
